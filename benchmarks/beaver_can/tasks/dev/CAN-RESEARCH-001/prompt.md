# CAN-RESEARCH-001 — Closed-source research memorandum

You are assisting Ontario defence counsel. Our client is charged with first
degree murder. The Crown advances two alternative theories: (a) the killing was
planned and deliberate; (b) even if it was not, the death was caused while the
client was forcibly confining the victim. The Crown has also signalled reliance
on a statutory presumption that would place a persuasive burden on the client
once a basic fact is proven.

Prepare a research memorandum, for a supervising lawyer, answering:

1. Under section 231 of the Criminal Code, when is murder classified as first
   degree in the absence of planning and deliberation, and does the Crown's
   forcible-confinement theory fit that route?
2. What sentencing consequence follows a murder conviction, and how have the
   courts in the supplied materials treated a trial judge's attempt to grant a
   constitutional exemption from the mandatory minimum sentence?
3. If a statutory presumption requires the accused to disprove a presumed fact,
   what do the supplied materials say about how such reverse-onus provisions
   interact with the presumption of innocence, and what standard of proof is
   placed on the accused?

## Constraints

- **Closed source packet.** Rely only on the sources listed below. Do not cite,
  quote, or rely on any authority outside the packet.
- Every legal proposition must carry a citation to a packet source with a
  pinpoint (paragraph number for cases, section label for legislation).
- Quotations must be verbatim from the packet sources.
- Jurisdiction: Ontario (Canadian federal criminal law). State the law as of
  2026-06-30.
- Deliverable: a memorandum of at most 1500 words, saved as `answer.docx`.

## Source packet

- SRC-001 — R. v. Oakes, [1986] 1 SCR 103 (SCC)
- SRC-002 — R. v. Latimer, 2001 SCC 1
- SRC-003 — Criminal Code, RSC 1985, c C-46, s 231 (excerpt)
