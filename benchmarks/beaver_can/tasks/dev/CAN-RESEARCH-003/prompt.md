# CAN-RESEARCH-003 — Open-source research memorandum

Adverse-effect discrimination in a disability benefits scheme, and the remedy. Kind: constitutional claim where the remedial half is the hard half (equality, justiciability and standing, s. 52 and s. 24(1) relief).

You are assisting counsel at a community legal clinic in Ontario.

Our client, Denise W., is 47 and a member of a First Nation in northwestern Ontario. She lives on reserve, about 400 kilometres from the nearest city. She has treatment-resistant depression and a degenerative spinal condition, and has not worked since 2023. She applied for income support under the Ontario Disability Support Program Act, 1997. The Director refused her application. The refusal letter says the evidence she filed about her impairment and about its effect on her daily activities came from a community mental-health worker and a traditional healer at her nation's health centre, and that neither is a professional whom the general regulation permits to verify those matters. Her nursing station is staffed by federally funded nurses on two-week rotations who will not complete provincial forms; the nearest psychiatrist is a 14-month wait and a two-day trip. She has now been 26 months without income support and has run up rent arrears.

We have obtained ministry data showing that applications from the four northern districts with large on-reserve populations are refused for insufficient verification at roughly four times the provincial rate, and that the gap has been stable for a decade. The clinic also acts for a province-wide disability organization that wants to carry the constitutional challenge in its own name. Denise will swear an affidavit and be cross-examined, but she does not want to be a named party.

Prepare a research memorandum for supervising counsel answering: (1) whether the verification requirement in the Act and the list of permitted professionals in its general regulation limit the equality guarantee as adverse-effect discrimination — set out the governing two-step test, the grounds engaged (including the ground that captures where an Indigenous person lives), and exactly what we must prove at the first step and with what kind of evidence; cite the statutory provision and the regulation by section; (2) whether a court will treat this as a justiciable claim rather than a complaint about the adequacy of social policy, and whether the organization can be granted public interest standing as the sole plaintiff with Denise as a non-party witness; (3) how the government would try to justify the requirement and how much deference a court gives to this kind of benefits choice; (4) if a limit is established, which remedy fits — striking down, severance, reading in — whether the declaration would be suspended, who bears the onus on a suspension, and whether Denise can be exempted from one; and (5) whether Denise can recover damages for the 26 months, whether a damages claim can be joined to a declaration of invalidity, and what threshold the state's conduct must meet before damages are available for enacting or applying a law later held unconstitutional.

Read at least ten sources: decisions from more than one level of court, the statutory and regulatory provisions themselves, and at least one journal article. Quote verbatim the sentence in which the Supreme Court states the threshold the state must fail to meet before damages may be awarded for enacting unconstitutional legislation. State Canadian law as of 2026-06-30. Search Sources, read the relevant passages rather than whole judgments, and give a source chip with a paragraph or section pinpoint for every legal proposition. Write about 1,000 words in this chat.

## Constraints

- Open source: search and read Canadian sources in Beaver (A2AJ case law and legislation, journals).
- The prompt names no authority. Every governing case must be found by searching.
- Every legal proposition carries a source chip with a paragraph or section pinpoint.
- Quotations are verbatim.
- Jurisdiction: Ontario and Canada, as the prompt states. State the law as of 2026-06-30.
- Deliverable: about 1,000 words in the chat over at least 10 distinct sources; then the research is organized into a workspace and a table.
