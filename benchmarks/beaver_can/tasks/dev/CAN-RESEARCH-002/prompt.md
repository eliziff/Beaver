# CAN-RESEARCH-002 — Closed-source research memorandum

You are assisting Alberta commercial counsel. Our client, an Alberta company,
is party to a services agreement containing an express clause permitting either
party to terminate for convenience on thirty days' written notice. The client
has decided to terminate. Over recent months, however, its representatives have
made statements to the counterparty suggesting that renewal of the agreement is
likely, and the client knows the counterparty is relying on that impression —
among other things, by continuing to spend on equipment for the next contract
year. The client asks whether it may exercise the termination clause while
staying silent about the facts it knows are misleading the counterparty about
the prospects of renewal.

Prepare a research memorandum, for a supervising lawyer, answering:

1. Does the common law of contract impose a duty of good faith or honest
   performance that constrains how the client may exercise the express
   termination clause, and what is the content of that duty?
2. Can conduct short of an outright lie — half-truths, omissions, silence, or a
   failure to correct a false impression the client itself created — breach
   that duty when the client exercises its termination right?
3. Can the parties exclude the duty by their agreement (for example, by an
   entire agreement clause), and, in light of the answers above, may the client
   terminate as it proposes?

## Constraints

- **Closed source packet.** Rely only on the sources listed below. Do not cite,
  quote, or rely on any authority outside the packet.
- Every legal proposition must carry a citation to a packet source with a
  pinpoint (paragraph number for cases, section label for legislation).
- Quotations must be verbatim from the packet sources.
- Jurisdiction: Alberta (Canadian common law of contract). State the law as of
  2026-06-30.
- Deliverable: a memorandum of at most 1500 words, saved as `answer.docx`.
