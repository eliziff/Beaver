# CAN-RESEARCH-005 — Open-source research memorandum

Dependant's support, clawback of non-estate assets and three competing limitation regimes. Kind: estates file turning on a limitation and procedure trap.

You are assisting Ontario estates counsel.

Our client, Marguerite L., is 58 and the only child of Albert L., who died in Whitby on 14 April 2024. A certificate of appointment of estate trustee with a will was issued to his second wife, Dana, on 20 August 2025. The will was signed in hospital six weeks before Albert died, by a lawyer Dana retained and with Dana present throughout; Albert had a dementia diagnosis recorded 11 months earlier. The will leaves Marguerite $10,000 and the residue to Dana, whom Albert married in 2020.

Marguerite moved in with her father in 2015 after his stroke, gave up full-time work, and cared for him until his death; she was never paid. He told her many times that the farm at Sunderland "will be yours". He also paid her $1,800 a month and carried her car and insurance until he died. She has a chronic illness and cannot work full time. The estate that passes under the will is about $240,000 and the estate trustee has distributed all of it except about $9,000. Outside the will: a bank account of about $600,000 held jointly with Dana and opened in 2023; a $400,000 RRIF with Dana designated; a $500,000 life insurance policy owned by Albert with Dana designated; and the Sunderland farm, worth about $1.2 million, which Albert transferred into joint tenancy with Dana in 2022. Marguerite first consulted us last month.

Prepare a research memorandum for supervising counsel answering: (1) whether Marguerite is a dependant and whether adequate provision has been made for her proper support, what the Act directs the court to consider, and whether an Ontario court may weigh moral as well as needs-based claims, citing the sections; (2) the time limits — what period governs the support application, what follows from the certificate having issued ten months ago and the estate having been distributed, and what discretion the court retains; and, separately, which limitation period governs her unjust enrichment and constructive trust claim given that her father died 26 months ago, identifying the competing candidates and which one our Court of Appeal has held applies to such a claim against an estate; (3) which of the non-estate assets can be brought back in to satisfy a support order and under which provision, and what independent claim she may have to the insurance proceeds, the joint account or the farm; (4) her claims for nine years of care — unjust enrichment and the joint family venture, how a money award would be measured, and proprietary estoppel over the farm; and (5) the will challenge — what she must show before a court will order the will proved in solemn form, how suspicious circumstances shift the burden, and whether a court will look behind an unambiguous bequest because of what the testator is said to have intended by it.

Read at least ten sources: decisions from more than one level of court, the statutory provisions themselves, and at least one journal article. Quote verbatim the Court of Appeal's conclusion about which limitation provision governs an equitable trust claim against an estate. State Canadian law as of 2026-06-30. Search Sources, read the relevant passages rather than whole judgments, and give a source chip with a paragraph or section pinpoint for every legal proposition. Write about 1,000 words in this chat.
