EXAMINATION FOR DISCOVERY OF RAYMOND OKAFOR held at Toronto, Ontario, on February 4, 2026, in the matter of Cedar Ridge Aggregates Inc. v. Bellwether Haulage Ltd., Court File No. CV-25-00714392.

MS. LEBLANC: Mr. Okafor, I want to take you through the delivery schedule for the third quarter.

THE WITNESS: Certainly.

MS. LEBLANC: You were the dispatcher on duty for the Cedar Ridge account through the summer of 2025, were you not?

THE WITNESS: I was, from the end of May until the middle of September.

MS. LEBLANC: And your responsibilities included recording the arrival time of each load at the Cedar Ridge yard.

THE WITNESS: Recording the arrival, and confirming the tonnage against the ticket the driver handed in.

[page 9]

MS. LEBLANC: Let me ask you about the site meeting. There was a meeting at the Cedar Ridge yard in August of 2025 to discuss the shortfalls, correct?

THE WITNESS: There was. The site meeting was on August 14, 2025, in the trailer at the north end of the yard.

MS. LEBLANC: Who attended that site meeting?

THE WITNESS: Myself, the yard foreman for Cedar Ridge, and our operations manager. Three of us in total.

MS. LEBLANC: And nothing was reduced to writing at the site meeting?

THE WITNESS: Not at the meeting itself. I made a note in the dispatch log afterwards.

[page 10]

MS. LEBLANC: I want to move to the tickets themselves. You produced a bundle of delivery tickets for the third quarter.

THE WITNESS: We produced everything the office had on file.

MS. LEBLANC: Were the tickets kept in date order?

THE WITNESS: They were filed by week, and within the week by truck number rather than by date.

MS. LEBLANC: So a ticket for a load delivered on a Friday might sit ahead of one delivered on the Monday of the same week.

THE WITNESS: That is possible, yes, if the Friday load came off a lower numbered truck.

[page 11]

MR. DUBOIS: I object to the form of that question. Counsel is asking the witness to speculate about the filing practices of a department he did not supervise.

MS. LEBLANC: I will rephrase. Mr. Okafor, from your own observation, were the tickets within a week kept in truck number order?

THE WITNESS: From what I saw when I pulled tickets, yes, they were in truck number order.

MS. LEBLANC: Thank you. Now, the load that is at the centre of this action is the load recorded on ticket 4471.

THE WITNESS: That is the one the parties have been arguing about.

[page 12]

MS. LEBLANC: Take your time and tell me everything you remember about the delivery on ticket 4471.

THE WITNESS: The truck was booked for an early run because the plant wanted the aggregate on site before the pour. The driver called in from the pit at about six in the morning to say he was loaded and rolling. I logged that call. He then hit the closure on the county road and had to go around by the highway, which cost him the better part of two hours, so what should have been a mid morning arrival became an afternoon arrival. When he finally got to the Cedar Ridge yard the foreman had gone for the day and the scale house was shut, so the driver left the load on the apron and dropped his ticket in the

[page 13]

box outside the office. The ticket he dropped was stamped by the scale house the following morning, which is why the tonnage entry and the arrival entry are on different lines in the log. The delivery itself was on August 14, 2025, and the stamp is the morning after. I remember it because it was the same week as the closure on the county road, and the closure ran from the Monday to the Thursday.

MS. LEBLANC: Was any part of that load rejected?

THE WITNESS: A portion was rejected on the following inspection, but not on arrival.

[page 14]

MS. LEBLANC: Those are my questions on the delivery. I will move to the invoicing.

MR. DUBOIS: Before you do, I want the record to reflect that the witness has been on the record for two hours and twenty minutes.

MS. LEBLANC: So noted. We will take the morning break and resume at eleven fifteen.

Whereupon the examination adjourned at 11:02 a.m. and resumed at 11:17 a.m.
